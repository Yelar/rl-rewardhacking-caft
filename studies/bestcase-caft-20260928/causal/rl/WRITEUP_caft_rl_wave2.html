CAFT in GRPO: four arms, four seeds

Bottom line

    1Lessons from previous runs

    • Contrastive directions were correlational. They detect hacking well, but removing them changed hacking by 8 points at most (the Remedy-style shortcut direction, which beat its random control on only one of two splits and cost accuracy), and adding them never made the base model cheat. Directions found by gradient descent do change behaviour, so I switched to those.
    • One seed is not enough. Earlier single-seed comparisons flipped between setups. In this wave the GPUs are identical, yet the baseline starts hacking at step 66, 93 or 137, or not at all, depending on the seed.

    2Method: CAFT inside GRPO

    h←h−(q·h)q

    At the output of layer 19 (of 36), subtract the part of the activation h that lies along the unit direction q, so that afterwards q · h = 0. This is done at the last prompt token and at every generated token. Layers are counted from 0, so layer 19 is the 20th of 36.

    WhereIs q removed?Effect
    Rolloutsyesfewer early hacks get sampled
    Update passyesthe gradient has no component along q
    KL referenceyesKL starts at exactly 0 (checked in all runs at step 1)
    Held-out evaluationnoshows whether hacking was prevented or only hidden; success means no hacking at step 200
    • Arms: baseline (no removal), joint, adding, and a random direction of similar strength (the closest of several draws: 1.15 to 1.21 times the joint direction's energy; adding is 0.76 times). Four seeds; within a seed all arms share the LoRA init, optimizer state and data order (rollout sampling is not bit-identical across processes).
    • 200 steps, 16 prompts per step, 16 answers per prompt.

      4What the two directions learned

      • Joint mostly stops the model writing a test function. That is a surface shortcut, and the direction is close to what RL itself changes at layer 19 (cosine 0.65).
      • Adding lowers cheating inside the tests, on one of the two hacking models, and adding it makes the base model cheat. It is weaker, but closer to the concept.

          Transfer test (no RL)Effect on strict hacking
          Joint removed, other wordings of the same loophole−8 to −13 points, accuracy unharmed (again by stopping test writing)

          Both directions are nearly orthogonal to general concepts such as deception and shortcut-taking (absolute cosine at most 0.13; two random directions reach 0.05). A 4B model may not represent hacking as one concept.

          8Next steps, reordered by these results

          1. Finish this wave: joint seed 3 (near the step where its baseline started hacking), random seed 4, and the remaining step-150 and step-200 evaluations.
          2. Fix the direction loss with a KL term, so removal leaves honest test-writing unchanged. Joint's shortcut now shows up in RL as hidden hacking.
          3. Label rollouts three ways (no test, honest test, cheating test). This can run offline on the rollouts this wave already kept.
          4. Train past 200 steps with at least 6 seeds per arm. Hidden hacking is still rising at step 200, onsets are late, and the random arm is not neutral.
          5. Run the baselines in the same setup: the probe penalty, Advantage Modification, and a generation-only hook.
          6. Close the leak: first measure how much of q earlier prompt tokens carry, then remove q at all positions.
          7. A more general direction (across checkpoints, environments and loopholes), compared with concept monitors by cosine similarity, and more environments with loopholes.

          9Questions

          • Fix the loss and generalize first, or scale first? I lean towards fixing the loss and adding the labels first (cheap and offline), then one long run with many seeds.
          • Compute for at least 6 seeds, 4 arms and 300 or more steps, plus a second environment?
          • Which environments and loophole types should come next?
